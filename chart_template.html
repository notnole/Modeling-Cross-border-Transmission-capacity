Capacity-Margrabe · monthly PTR bid rule

Does the fair-value bid earn anything the auction wasn’t already giving away?

Every JAO monthly capacity auction on the — corridors this model can price — — directed zone pairs, with the German-Czech border splitting into four TSO-level instruments — 2024-08 → 2026-07. Each right is valued at its own bid gate closure — a median 12 days before delivery starts — from the Margrabe spread option fitted on the 12 whole months of day-ahead history before it. We bid the model’s fair value and pay the clearing price when we win. Plotted against the two references that matter: buying every right at any price, and perfect foresight, which buys only the rights that turned out profitable.

Read this first

P&L is EUR at 1 MW on each border: (realised value − clearing price) × delivery hours. Realised value is mean(max(P_to − P_from, 0)) over the delivered hours — a non-nominated right is paid the coupling differential under UIOSI, so a negative spread pays 0, not a loss.

One thing is deliberately not modelled: we assume a price taker — our bid never moves the clearing price. Plausible at 1 MW against 30-plus-bidder books, but unverified.

Maintenance is not a discount. DE(50Hz)-CZ averages 172 h a month of reduced ATC and its maintenance_min_atc_mw is 0, so even 1 MW is physically out — but under HAR Art 59 a curtailed holder is made whole at the same max(spread, 0) a non-nominated holder receives, and all 7,728 observed curtailment records read Network Security, not force majeure. So value per MWh is not reduced. The residual is the Art 59(2)–(4) compensation cap, which cuts all holders pro rata and is not quantifiable without JAO’s cap figures — it would bite hardest on exactly these corridors.

CZ-DE and DE-CZ run no monthly auction under those names, but the TSO-split corridors do and price off the same DE_LU zone. An earlier version of this page dropped them; they turned out to be the largest single source of P&L here.

Profitability per delivery month

Bars are summed across the corridors offered in that month, so a month where the rule buys nothing sits at zero rather than going missing. The ceiling is a bound, not a rival strategy — it is what the same rights were worth to someone who already knew.

Cumulative, and where it all comes from

The dashed line is the same rule with both rich border groups removed — DE-AT/AT-DE and the four German-Czech corridors. Those two groups carry the entire result: strip them and the rule is a loss, and bidding indiscriminately on either was already profitable without any model. Excluding only DE-AT, as an earlier version of this page did, understates the rule — the German-Czech corridors are the larger source. The gap between the rule and the ceiling is the honest measure of how much of the available money the fair value actually finds.

Against the fundamental model

The sibling capacities project attacks the same auction from the opposite direction: per-zone LGBM day-ahead price forecasts off 64 Volue fundamentals curves, turned into a predicted spread and bid at its p50 with a calibration factor. No forwards, no fitted volatility. Both bid books overlap on , and both are scored here against this project’s realised values — the two agree to 0.016 EUR/MWh on average anyway, computing the same payoff from independent price pulls.

How accurate is each value estimate?

Rule-independent: just the predicted value against what the right turned out to be worth. Shorter is better. The forward spread floored at zero is the do-nothing reference.

And what does each bid rule earn?

Cumulative, same auctions, same target. Both models clear zero on this set and the forward-vol one clears it by 3x, but the interesting line is both agree — buy only where the forward-vol model and the fundamental model independently say the right is worth more than it cleared. Because JAO is marginal-priced, that is exactly the same thing as submitting one order at the lower of the two fair values: min(a,b) ≥ c if and only if both clear c, and you pay c either way. Verified identical — same win set, same P&L to the cent.

Lower, average, or max of the two fair values?

Marginal pricing means the bid level does nothing except select the win set, so these three rules are nested: everything the lower bid buys, the average buys, and everything the average buys, the max buys. That makes each relaxation testable on its own terms — the bottom two rows price only the auctions a looser rule adds.

Where the money is: agreement, not either model

Mean P&L per auction in each cell of the 2×2. Disagreement is the signal to stay out: whenever one model buys and the other declines, the average auction loses money.
